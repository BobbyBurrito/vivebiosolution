JANITORIAL PURCHASING GUIDE

How Businesses Can Reduce Janitorial Supply Costs Without Sacrificing Quality

Lowering janitorial cost is not about finding the cheapest case on a price sheet. A useful comparison looks at the complete purchasing and usage picture.

VIVE BIOSOLUTION · Updated July 31, 2026 · Practical commercial cleaning guide

The easiest way to make janitorial purchasing look cheaper is to compare one case price and ignore everything around it. That is also how businesses end up with “savings” that disappear through freight, pack-size differences, poor product equivalency or extra labour.

Compare Equivalent Products First

A quote is only useful when the products being compared are reasonably equivalent for the intended application. Check dimensions, material, case count, concentration, dispenser compatibility and other specifications that materially affect use. A lower price on a smaller case or different specification is not a real saving.

Compare Cost Per Usable Unit

For consumables, compare cost per bag, roll, mop head or other usable unit. For concentrated chemicals, compare the cost of the usable solution at the intended dilution. This prevents packaging differences from distorting the comparison.

Include Freight and Minimum Orders

A supplier can appear inexpensive until delivery charges, minimum orders or split-order fees are added. Ask what the delivered cost looks like at your normal purchasing frequency, not only on an unusually large order.

Do Not Ignore Labour

Labour often costs more than the chemical used during a cleaning task. If a product requires repeated applications, excessive rinsing or significantly more scrubbing, the lowest chemical price may not produce the lowest operating cost.

Vendor Consolidation Can Have Value

Buying chemicals, liners, mops, paper products and related supplies through fewer purchasing relationships can reduce the number of orders, invoices and vendor conversations a facility manages. Consolidation only makes sense, however, if pricing, availability and product suitability remain competitive.

A useful supplier comparison should include:

Comparable specification · pack size · unit cost · freight · minimum order · availability · lead time · chemical dilution where applicable · service requirements.

Use Your Existing Supply List as the Starting Point

You do not need to redesign your janitorial program just to obtain a competitive quote. A practical approach is to send the current item descriptions or SKUs, pack sizes, approximate quantities and delivery location. A supplier can then identify comparable options where available.

Frequently Asked Questions

Should we choose the janitorial supplier with the lowest case price?

Not by case price alone. Compare equivalent specifications, pack sizes, delivered cost, availability and cost per usable unit.

Can one supplier provide cleaning chemicals and janitorial consumables?

Yes. Some suppliers can consolidate chemicals, liners, mops, paper products and related facility consumables. Confirm which products are manufactured directly and which are sourced.

What information is useful for a comparative janitorial quote?

Current item descriptions or SKUs, pack sizes, approximate quantities, order frequency and delivery location make the comparison substantially more useful.
